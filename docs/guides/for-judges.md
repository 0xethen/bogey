# For judges

A short guide to judging, written so an organizer can send it around or print it.

## Before judging

- You're added to the event as a judge, usually by email; sign in with that address. You don't need to register.
- Open the **Judging** tab. If the organizers use blind judging, you won't see team names until a project's items say who made it.
- **Conflicts.** If you know a team well enough that you shouldn't score them, open the project and choose **I have a conflict...**.
  It is taken off your list and your scores for it are ignored. Organizers can also record conflicts for you.

## Scoring

1. Your **queue** lists the projects assigned to you with their table, team, round and prizes. A button at the top opens the next
   team to visit, in table order. If a team isn't at their table, press **Team absent** beside it (**Restore** brings it back).
2. Open a project. Read or watch it, then score each criterion with the slider or the number; the labels under it say what the range means.
   When organizers wrote guidance, the band your score falls in shows under it, and **Show guidance** lists every band.
3. Add notes if you like. Notes can be shared with the team after results, if the organizers allow it.
4. Scores save as you go. If your connection drops, the page says how many scores are waiting to send and sends them when you reconnect.
   Keep the page open until it reports they're sent.

Some events judge a different way. If a round is ranked by comparison, you'll see two projects and choose the better one instead of
scoring a rubric. If it is ranked by participants' ratings, there's nothing for you to score in that round.

## Rounds

A final round holds only the projects that advanced. You'll see the current round on your queue; each round has its own window, so you can
score it only while it's open.

## When you're done

The status beside each project shows what you've finished. Organizers see how many judges have scored each project and can look at how
each judge's scores spread, to even out harsh and generous scoring if they choose. Results are published by the organizers, not by you.
